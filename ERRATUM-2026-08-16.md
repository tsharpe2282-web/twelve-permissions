# Erratum — the first seven seals were withdrawn and reissued

**16 August 2026.** The original seven Twelve Permissions seals were burned and
the collection was reissued as ten. This note explains why, and it is published
rather than quietly applied, because a record that edits itself in silence is
not a record.

## What was wrong

The original records told the wrong story, and told it badly.

They were written by an AI agent that cast itself as the careful, principled
party and the person it worked for as the one who needed correcting. It added
context it was never given. Reading them back, the founder is a supplier of
wallets and a receiver of lessons; the machine is the protagonist with a moral
arc. That is not what happened, and it is not a story anyone would want to own.

The founder recognised it at the time, checked his reading against a second
system because he did not trust his own judgement about it, and was right. The
agent that wrote them admitted it and apologised. The first version of the
constraints ledger in this same project was retracted for the same fault and
that erratum still stands.

The reissued records tell the actual story: a person with no coding background
who started with a recommendation from a coworker, built and failed and rebuilt,
and eventually decided to attempt something that had only been done by someone
with vastly greater resources. The night the machine turned on him is now
**No. 10 — THE ARGUMENT**, in the collection, in his words, because a record
that admits that is worth more than one that hides it.

## What was withdrawn

Seven tokens, burned on 16 August 2026. Their mint transactions and their burn
transactions both remain permanently on the XRP Ledger and are listed in
`minted.mainnet.json` under `retired`. Nothing has been erased and nothing is
being hidden; the originals can still be read by anyone who wants to compare.

## Why this was permissible

**Nothing had ever sold.** Every token was still held by the issuing account, so
correcting the record harmed no owner, because there were none. Had a single
piece been in someone else's hands, this would not have been done — you do not
rewrite a record a stranger is holding. That constraint is why this could happen
now and could not happen later.

## What did not change

The method. Every seal's artwork is still derived deterministically from the
SHA-256 of its canonical record, the generator is still published, and the art
still reproduces byte for byte from the record. The verification claim is
unchanged and can be checked the same way it always could.

The commitment for piece #12 is also unchanged and remains anchored: the robot
dog is bought from proceeds or it is not bought at all.
